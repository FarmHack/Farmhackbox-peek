    Every tool here, placed in Mirabeau's tree

    Point at any hexagon and it names the tool; click it and it opens — on a running box, the app itself; in this preview, its page here or a note on what it launches. A shelf of source texts and a box full of running software are usually two separate facts about the same machine. This is the one picture where they are the same fact. Each hexagon is a component this box actually runs, coloured by the stratum it observes and carrying the mark of what it does with what it sees — set into the structure the tenth text on this shelf describes.

    “The State is a tree: the roots are Agriculture, the trunk is Population, the branches are Industry, the leaves are Commerce properly so called and the Arts.”

    Mirabeau, L'Ami des hommes (1756) — read from the 1756 scan, not from a quote site. Our translation. Full entry, with the French and the provenance, on the shelf below.

    Read as a tree it says what the box is for; read as an inventory it says what the box is missing. Eighteen cells in the trunk and one in the water. A state in decline, he writes, is raised up neither by commerce nor by the arts — that is watering the tree by its leaves. The argument is his; the numbers are ours.
